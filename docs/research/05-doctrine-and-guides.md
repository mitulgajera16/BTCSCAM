## PART 1 — PARAS CHOPRA'S FRAMEWORKS, VERIFIED

Nine frameworks traced to specific essays with verbatim passages. Everything that could not be traced is in the excluded list below — no padding.

### F1. Businesses fulfil existing desires; startups capture demand, they don't create it — VERIFIED

**Source:** "Businesses exist to fulfil human desires" — https://invertedpassion.com/why-do-businesses-exist/

Chopra argues human desires were fixed by evolution long before any product existed; capitalism is the ongoing competition to satisfy those unchanged desires better, cheaper, faster. "Human desires have been shaped by millions of years of evolutionary programming and therefore haven't changed much during the course of history." … "Startups succeed because people are forever looking to fulfil their desires better than before." … "What fuels capitalism is not the shiny new thing but the old one done in a better way." His operating question: "Always ask yourself what human desire is your business fulfilling and how can you fulfil it better, cheaper and faster than current alternatives?"

**BTCSCAM application:** The desires are already documented and already being acted on: protect-the-next-victim fused with shame-conversion ("I know I'm dumb but let me put this out so no one else falls for it"), the desire to be heard (the IC3 black-hole complaint), and recognition (Bitcointalk hunters' three currencies). The community phase must not manufacture a new desire ("join our community!"). It should answer Chopra's question literally: reporters already fulfil the warning desire by hand-writing keyword-stuffed Reddit posts — BTCSCAM fulfils the same desire better (a structured report page that ranks harder, persists, and reports back status). Same desire, superior fulfilment.

### F2. Evidence of desire is behavior, not words — VERIFIED

**Source:** "Evidence of desire is in people's behavior (and not in what they say)" — https://invertedpassion.com/evidence-of-desire-customer-behavior/

People's stated preferences are social performance; their actions reveal what they actually want. "We, humans, are notorious for saying something but doing something else." … "Customers vote with their wallets." … "Behavior, not words, is the real evidence of desire."

**BTCSCAM application:** Agent 3's evidence is precisely the kind Chopra trusts — observed behavior, not surveys: ≥400 "is this a scam" posts/month in r/Scams; reporters hand-writing SEO warnings unprompted; hunters volunteering hundreds of hours for thanks and takedown confirmations. That is behavioral proof the demand exists. The flip side is a warning: do not build for what victims *say* (e.g., anything smelling of money recovery — reporters explicitly disclaim it in behavior). Build only for the behaviors already on display: checking, warning, wanting status.

### F3. Be in the desires market, not the solutions market — VERIFIED

**Source:** "Be in the desire market, not the solutions market" — https://invertedpassion.com/jobs-to-be-done/

Desires are permanent, solutions are temporary, and all solutions competing for a desire compete with each other. "What people care about is their desires and not how they're currently fulfilling them. If a better way to fulfill their desire comes along, they'll switch to it in a jiffy." … "No matter how unique, all new things compete with old things."

**BTCSCAM application:** BTCSCAM's real competitors are not other scam databases. For the checking desire, the incumbent "solution" is asking strangers on Reddit one thread at a time (majority get zero replies). For the reporting desire, it's IC3/FTC silence. For the warning desire, it's a keyword-stuffed post that may never rank. Positioning, content, and SEO should target the desire's own language ("is [X] a scam", the victim's hand-written phrase grammar) — not category language ("crypto scam database"), which nobody's desire speaks in.

### F4. Behavior change is expensive — ride existing habits, ask for tiny changes — VERIFIED (two essays)

**Sources:** "How does behavior change happen" — https://invertedpassion.com/how-does-behavior-change-happen/ ; "Habits prevent people from switching from the familiar to the new" — https://invertedpassion.com/habits-and-intertia/

"Behavior change is very hard. People know what is good for them, they're motivated to do those things, and yet aren't able to get things done." His prescription: "What we should do instead is ask them to make a tiny change, which is easy to perform, and then give them hope that we will build upon this tiny thing over time." And on switching: "It's a myth that people are on a constant lookout to (marginally) improve their lives." … "a good thumb rule is to aim for at least 2x improvement over the existing solutions on parameters that customers care about."

**BTCSCAM application:** Never ask victims or reporters to adopt a new ritual. Intercept the three habits they already have: (1) googling the exact scam phrase → report pages engineered to rank for the victim's own phrase grammar; (2) posting "is this a scam" on r/Scams → be present in that channel and absorb the zero-reply majority rather than demand they come to us first; (3) filing with IC3 → piggyback ("filed with IC3? Post it here too — here you'll see what happens next"). The 2x test is easily passed on the parameter victims care most about: any visible status beats permanent silence not by 2x but by infinity — silence is the baseline.

### F5. Most users are lazy (Marl); build the community around the non-lazy minority — VERIFIED (two essays)

**Sources:** "Assume most people are lazy but market to those who aren't" — https://invertedpassion.com/assume-most-people-are-lazy-but-market-to-those-who-arent/ ; "Making a product that Marl loves" — https://invertedpassion.com/making-a-product-that-marl-loves/

"Every market is comprised of a small segment of such early adopters." … "The *only* job of a marketer at a startup boils down to finding early adopters and telling them what's new on offer." … "All humans like to boast and early adopters are no exception to this." And on the median user: "The average marginal user of mobile apps is minimally committed, highly distracted, constantly craving dopamine hits and always ready to ditch apps after a microsecond of boredom."

**BTCSCAM application:** Two different surfaces for two different humans. The contribute surface is for the non-lazy minority — Bitcointalk-style veteran hunters and shame-converting reporters — and should pay their three verified currencies (visible outcomes, public thanks, investigator identity/reputation). Chopra's "humans like to boast" is the doctrine's permission slip for investigator credits and public track records: hunters who boast are distribution. The check surface is for Marl — a distracted, frightened person mid-scam who needs a one-line verdict above the fold, evidence below. Do not make Marl read a dossier; do not make the hunter feel like Marl.

### F6. What kills startups is the lack of feedback — VERIFIED

**Source:** "What kills startups is the lack of feedback" — https://invertedpassion.com/what-kills-startups-is-the-lack-of-feedback/

"What you should fear is silence. If no feedback is coming your way, prospects are not replying to emails, or users are dropping off without telling you why then there's simply no way for you to iterate." … "It's extremely demotivating to keep putting effort without getting any feedback. At least with negative feedback, there's a feeling of the right struggle." … "Nothing succeeds like the feeling that you're succeeding."

**BTCSCAM application:** This one cuts twice. First, it *is* the product thesis: the #1 complaint about official channels (report → silence forever) is exactly the failure mode Chopra says kills motivation — IC3 makes every reporter feel like a failing startup. BTCSCAM's core loop is therefore a feedback machine: every report gets a status, every status change gets surfaced, every takedown gets a confirmation and a thank-you. "Nothing succeeds like the feeling that you're succeeding" is precisely why Bitcointalk hunters work for takedown confirmations. Second, internally: every community-phase experiment must emit measurable feedback weekly, or it's silently dying.

### F7. Never stop creating network effects; stack positive feedback loops — VERIFIED (two essays)

**Sources:** "Never stop creating network effects in your business" — https://invertedpassion.com/never-stop-creating-network-effects-in-your-business/ ; "How to create legal monopolies via network effects and economic moats" — https://invertedpassion.com/network-effects-economic-moats/

"A business is said to have network effects if each new customer increases the value derived by all other customers." … "Positive feedback loops gradually build unbreachable moats for a business. So you must strive to find not just one positive feedback loop in your business, but as many as you possibly can." On switching costs: "Make the customers invested in your product to the level that it becomes really expensive for them to make a switch."

**BTCSCAM application:** BTCSCAM has a natural data network effect — each report makes every future check more likely to hit (Chopra's Google example: more data → better results for *all* users). Stack the loops deliberately: (1) report → ranking page → next victim lands → reports; (2) takedown confirmation → credibility → faster registrar cooperation → more confirmations; (3) investigator reputation accrued on-site → hunters' identity becomes expensive to abandon (the switching-cost moat, applied to contributors instead of customers). ScamAdviser has loop 1 only, and poisoned it with colonized reviews.

### F8. Don't be a first mover; markets are co-created, and failed competitors are proof of desire — VERIFIED

**Source:** "Don't be a first-mover, be the first one to get it right" — https://invertedpassion.com/dont-be-a-first-mover/

"Any one person or one company cannot create a new market. Markets are usually co-created by multiple competitors offering to satisfy a particular customer desire." … "be an early mover, but don't be a first mover as the lack of competition most likely signifies a lack of genuine customer desire."

**BTCSCAM application:** ScamAdviser, BBB Scam Tracker, Chainabuse, and r/Scams itself are the co-creators — they proved the desire and taught users the checking behavior. Their documented failures (false positives AND negatives, review sections colonized by recovery scammers, zero-reply threads) are the "get it right" brief, not discouraging competition. BTCSCAM's position is the second mover that fixes the trust failure: human-verified dossiers, no pay-to-remove, visibly moderated outcomes.

### F9. All new products compete with attention; reach fewer people, repeatedly — VERIFIED

**Source:** "All new products compete with Instagram for attention" — https://invertedpassion.com/all-products-compete-with-instagram/

"all marketing messages compete for attention with however people want to spend their attention" … "Effective marketers would rather reduce the number of people they reach but they ensure that they reach them again and again via multiple channels."

**BTCSCAM application:** BTCSCAM's structural luck: it doesn't have to beat Instagram for idle attention, because its moment of need comes with hyper-focused attention — a frightened person actively googling a phrase at 2am. So spend zero effort on broad awareness; spend everything on being present at the three high-motivation moments (the search, the r/Scams post, the post-report despair), repeatedly, in the same narrow channels. This is also the doctrine argument for guides-as-distribution: guides are the durable interception surface for those moments.

## So what for BTCSCAM

- The doctrine in one line: **every verified Chopra framework says ride, never invent** — and Agent 3 proved the riders exist. The community phase is three existing behaviors given a better vehicle: the hand-written SEO warning becomes a structured report page; the zero-reply Reddit check becomes a guaranteed-response check; the IC3 black hole becomes a visible status ladder.
- The product's soul is F6: BTCSCAM is the anti-black-hole. "Nothing succeeds like the feeling that you're succeeding" is the design spec for reporter retention — status changes, takedown confirmations, and thanks are not features, they are the compensation.
- Two-surface architecture (F5): a Marl-grade check surface (instant verdict) and a hunter-grade contribute surface (identity, reputation, outcomes). Don't average them into one mediocre page.
- Guides are not a content side-quest; they are F9's interception surfaces and F7's loop-starters — searcher → checker → reporter → data → better checks.
- Publish guides 1+3 as a pair inside the Coldcard news window; 2 for mission differentiation; 4 to open the funnel cluster. Inheritance waits until the disclosure pattern is proven.
